One fire record from MODIS and VIIRS, from 2000 to today.

Challenge: Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots

Data: NASA FIRMS1 / 7

The problem

Two sensors count the same fires on two different scales

MODIS has watched fires since 2000, VIIRS since 2012. VIIRS has finer pixels and sees about four times more hot spots.

Join the two records as they are, and fire activity seems to jump in 2012. No trend that crosses that year can be trusted.

×3.80 jump at 2012 in a naive splice, worldwide
×0.97 the same ratio in the harmonized record

Northern California, detections per year. Dashed blue: MODIS as recorded. Dashed purple: VIIRS as recorded, from 2012. Solid orange: the harmonized record, with no step at 2012.

The solution

One unit for 26 years, shown as a calendar for any area

  1. All FIRMS detections of MODIS and VIIRS since November 2000 go onto one 0.1° grid.
  2. Coefficients for every 1° cell convert MODIS to the scale of VIIRS Suomi NPP. They are fitted on 2012 to 2020, while both sensors flew and MODIS kept its nominal orbits.
  3. Draw an area and read its record: a row per year, a column per month, with the likely range of every value.

The solution

Is this month unusual for this place?

The current month is compared with the same days of every earlier year, on one scale. Northern California, November 1 to 13, 2026: 59 % above the median.

  • Any area: draw it on the map or type its coordinates.
  • Deeper views: anomalies, daily calendar, hot spots of a month.
  • Take it with you: CSV of hot spots and tables, charts as PNG.
  • For everyone: phone, keyboard, color-blind-safe scales.

NASA data

Built on NASA FIRMS, checked on years the method has never seen

  • MODIS Terra since November 2000, Aqua since 2002: Collection 6, then 6.1 from 2023.
  • VIIRS 375 m Collection 2, Suomi NPP from 2012 to November 2026; NOAA-20 since 2018, the lead sensor from November 2026.
  • 2026 through the FIRMS Area API: standard data to July, near real time after it.
  • NASA GIBS Blue Marble imagery under the map.

Every detection of 26 years is kept: about 9 GB of archives from NASA FIRMS, on a 0.1° grid.

Fitted on 2012 to 2020. For 2021 to 2025 VIIRS is predicted from MODIS alone and compared with what VIIRS really recorded.

Raw MODIS, without coefficients, runs about 70 % below VIIRS. The limits of the method are listed in the app, next to the results.

What is next

From a calendar to an early-warning tool

  • Automatic daily updates: today new FIRMS detections are added by hand; next is a daily job that takes them from the FIRMS API.
  • Alerts for an area: a notice when the current month leaves its normal range.
  • A calendar of burning intensity: hot spots on the map are already colored by fire radiative power and the API gives the harmonized FRP calendar; next is a switch for it in the calendar.
  • Finer coefficients for small areas, and NOAA-21 as the next sensor of the record.
